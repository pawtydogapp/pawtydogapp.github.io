How long should puppy walks be?

Pawty blog · 5 minute read

New puppy owners hear 2 opposite instructions in week 1: a tired dog is a good dog, and do not over-exercise a growing puppy. Both are true, and the bridge between them is a simple rule of thumb.

The 5-minute rule

A common guideline used by vets and trainers: about 5 minutes of structured leash walking per month of age, up to 2 times per day. A 3-month puppy gets roughly 15 minutes, twice. A 6-month puppy around 30 minutes, twice. It is a rough guide, not a law, but it protects the thing that matters most: growth plates in the joints, which stay soft until roughly 12 months in small breeds and up to 18 months in large ones.

What counts, and what does not

The limit is about forced marching on a leash at human pace. Free sniffing, gentle play and garden pottering are far softer on joints, and sniffing in particular is a workout for the brain. Ten minutes of serious sniffing tires many dogs more than an hour of trotting. What to avoid until the puppy is grown: long stair sessions, jumping down from heights, and running next to a bike.

Read the puppy, not the clock

Lying down mid-walk, lagging behind, or heavy panting means the walk is over, wherever you are. Carrying a tired puppy home is not spoiling, it is good management. Puppies also sleep 18 to 20 hours a day, and a lot of week-1 biting and chaos is simply an overtired puppy that needed a nap, not a walk.

As they grow

Adult dogs mostly thrive on 2 walks of 30 to 60 minutes plus dedicated sniffing time, adjusted for breed and age. Summer note: test asphalt with the back of your hand for 5 seconds before walking. Winter note: rinse road salt off the paws.
