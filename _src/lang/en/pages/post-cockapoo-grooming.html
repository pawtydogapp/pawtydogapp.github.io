Cockapoo grooming: what nobody tells you before you buy one

Grooming · 6 minute read

Nala is a cockapoo. She is the reason half the exercises in Pawty were rewritten, and she is also the reason this article exists.

Cockapoos, cavapoos, labradoodles, bichons, poodles and most other curly or wavy crosses share one thing that is rarely mentioned at the point of sale: the coat never stops growing. It does not shed out. It has to be cut, for the rest of the dog's life, every 6 to 10 weeks.

The number to know

A professional groom costs roughly 40 to 80 EUR across Europe and is needed every 6 to 10 weeks. That is 260 to 690 EUR a year, for 12 to 15 years.

It is the second largest recurring cost after food, and unlike food it has no cheap option, because skipping it does not save money. It creates matting, and matting costs more.

Matting is the whole problem

A curly coat traps the hair it sheds instead of dropping it. That trapped hair tangles, then felts, then tightens against the skin.

Matted coat pulls constantly. It hides skin infections. It traps moisture. In bad cases the only humane option is to shave the whole dog to the skin, which most owners find upsetting and which no groomer enjoys doing.

Matting forms fastest in exactly the places people brush least: behind the ears, in the armpits, on the collar line, between the back legs, and around the tail.

What actually prevents it

Brushing to the skin, not over the top. This is the single mistake almost everyone makes. Running a brush across the surface leaves the coat underneath untouched, so the top looks perfect while a solid mat forms against the skin.

The method groomers use is line brushing:

  1. Part the coat with your free hand so you can see skin.
  2. Brush the small section from the skin outwards with a slicker brush.
  3. Move the parting up a centimetre and repeat.
  4. Work through the whole dog in lines.
  5. Then check with a metal comb. If the comb cannot pass from skin to tip, the brush did not do the job.

The comb is the test, the brush is the tool. If you only take one thing from this article, take that.

How often

  • Puppy coat, under 8 months: 10 minutes, 3 times a week. Easier than it will ever be again.
  • Coat change, 8 to 14 months: daily. The puppy coat comes out under the adult coat and mats faster than at any other time in the dog's life. Most owners are ambushed by this.
  • Adult coat: 15 minutes, 3 or 4 times a week, plus a full comb-through before and after anything wet or muddy.

Start handling in week 1

The nail trims and ear cleans start much later, but the tolerance for being handled has to be built long before you need it.

Every day, for 10 seconds each: hold a paw, look in an ear, touch the tail, run a hand down each leg. Treat after each one. That is the entire exercise, and it is why some dogs stand calmly at the groomer and others have to be muzzled.

Do the same with the brush before the coat needs brushing. Brush once, treat, stop. The dog should learn that the brush appears and something good happens, before it ever learns that the brush can pull.

Choosing and using a groomer

  • Book the first appointment at 4 to 5 months, even if the coat does not need it. The point is a positive first experience, not a haircut.
  • Ask for a puppy introduction session: bath, dry, tidy up, home. Short and easy.
  • Say the length you want in millimetres, not in adjectives. "Short" means different things to different people. A 10 millimetre body clip with a longer head is a common, practical cut.
  • A shorter cut in summer is not cruel. It is far kinder than a matted long one.
  • Do not blame the groomer for a shave-down. If the coat is matted to the skin, cutting it out is the only humane option and dematting a bad coat is painful.

The kit that matters

  • A slicker brush. The main tool.
  • A metal comb. The test. Non-negotiable.
  • Blunt-nosed scissors for around the eyes and paws between grooms.
  • A detangling spray, because brushing a dry coat breaks the hair.

Skip the deshedding blades sold for double-coated breeds. They are the wrong tool for a curly coat and they damage it.

The honest summary

Cockapoos are wonderful dogs and the coat is the price. If 300 to 700 EUR a year and 15 minutes of brushing several times a week does not fit your life, a short-coated dog will be a happier choice for both of you.

If it does fit, start the handling in week 1 and buy the comb.
