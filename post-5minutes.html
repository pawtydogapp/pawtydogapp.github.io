Why 5 minutes a day beats weekend marathons

Pawty blog · 3 minute read

The most common training mistake is not a technique. It is scheduling: nothing all week, then a 45-minute Sunday session that leaves both dog and human frustrated.

Dogs learn in repetitions, not hours

Most dogs need 20 to 40 rewarded repetitions before a new cue truly sticks, and studies on spaced learning show short, frequent sessions produce better retention than long blocks. A 3-minute session holds maybe 15 repetitions at full attention. Minute 30 of a marathon session holds none, the dog checked out long ago.

End on a win

Short sessions have a built-in superpower: it is easy to stop while things still work. Ending on a success means the dog's last memory of training is a good one, which is exactly what makes them enthusiastic tomorrow.

Make consistency painless

The hard part is not the 5 minutes, it is remembering daily. This is why we built streaks into Pawty, with a freeze instead of a reset when life happens, because guilt is a terrible training partner, for dogs and for humans.
